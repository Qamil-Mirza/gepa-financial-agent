## Design Context

### Users
Developers who run LLM agent workflows in production and plug PitStop into their code with a few lines. They open the dashboard in two moods: a routine check ("is everything healthy, am I on budget?") and an incident ("I just got paged, what broke, what changed, what do I do?"). The job to be done: see which workflows are drifting (off-task or degraded quality), how urgent it is (P0–P3, driven by the workflow's declared criticality), what likely caused it (model / provider / prompt change), what it is costing, and approve or roll back the proposed fix.

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, trustworthy. A well-run control room: quiet by default, and when something is wrong that one thing is unmissable. Plain language over jargon; every alert says what happened, why it matters, and what to do next. Emotional goal: confidence that nothing is silently burning money or going off the rails.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Identity:** keep the PitStop name with a subtle racing nod (flag-derived status colors, pit-wall vocabulary used sparingly). No heavy theming, no checkered patterns.
- **References:** Sentry / PagerDuty for incident-first clarity and obvious urgency; Vercel / Stripe for clean, editorial layout and generous whitespace.
- **Anti-references:** the generic AI dashboard look: neon-on-dark, purple/blue gradients, glowing glass cards, hero-metric tiles, decorative sparklines.
- **Theme:** light and dark, following the system setting with a manual toggle. Neutrals tinted (no pure black/white), status colors reserved for status.
- **Home screen:** a workflow health board (one entry per workflow: criticality, health, open drifts by P-level, spend vs hard budget), drilling into a workflow's drifts and "what changed" history.

### Design Principles
1. **Quiet until it matters.** Healthy things recede; color and weight are spent only on what needs attention, in P-level order.
2. **Urgency is never color alone.** Every P-level carries a text label; status is legible to color-blind users and in grayscale (WCAG AA contrast as the baseline — my call, adjust if needed).
3. **Every alert answers three questions:** what drifted, what likely changed, what's being done (halted / fell back / fix awaiting review).
4. **Money is first-class.** Spend against the hard budget is visible wherever a workflow appears.
5. **Calm motion.** State changes only, ease-out, respects reduced-motion.
